## Experiment

The split was declared before these fits: the third dither in each activity
group was withheld. The training set has **six R and six C exposures**; the test
set has **three R and three C exposures**, indices `[2,5,8,11,14,17]` in the
saved ordering. All 18 are distinct observations. Original masks are retained.
The native data have 77,308 selected training pixels and 38,636 withheld pixels.

The existing calibrated native forward model fits one flexible positive gas
map and one rotation field jointly. Gas brightness is independent of the stellar
brightness profile. The existing imaging inclination density is retained.
Intrinsic dispersion is fixed exactly to zero. No MCMC was run.

Only training science pixels enter the galaxy objective. Registration and
relative gains are fitted for training exposures; withheld registration is
fixed to zero and gains to one. The gain contrasts sum to zero over the training
set alone. An explicit check verifies zero held-out data and inverse-error
weights in the training likelihood.

Four starts were used: the previously saved lower and higher branches, and two
smooth-map starts at 200 and 800 km/s. The smooth starts use no saved galaxy
parameters. All four satisfy the declared first-order stationarity threshold;
this does not establish a global optimum. Saved full-data seeds and previous
model development mean this remains a **retrospective diagnostic**, not a fully
independent discovery experiment.

Spatial integration uses 121×121 nodes. At zero intrinsic dispersion, one
spectral node reproduces the saved 17-node prediction and objective to the
specified numerical tolerance, avoiding redundant identical spectral samples.
The GPU training worker took **12.64 seconds**, including reconstruction and
compilation. This is not an estimate of MCMC runtime. The four fits themselves
took approximately 8.5 seconds in total.

## Withheld observations

After training, gas maps, kinematics and physical line flux are frozen. The
withheld exposure continuum and background are estimated **only from sideband
pixels at least 28 detector pixels from the nominal line position along the
dispersion direction**. One identical continuum prediction is used for all
four candidate galaxy models. None of the scored central line pixels tunes
this continuum or any galaxy parameter.

Predicted galaxy-line contamination of the sidebands is negligible for both
branches: at most 5.1×10⁻⁸ of the modeled line flux per exposure. This does not
test contamination from other objects or the adequacy of the continuum family.

Continuum uncertainty is correlated across the predicted line pixels. We use
the sideband fit's active positive coefficients to propagate the full Gaussian
curvature covariance, including its covariance with the signed background:

\[
C = D + A H_{\rm side}^{-1} A^T,\qquad Q=r^T C^{-1}r.
\]

The same covariance applies to both candidates, so its determinant cancels in
their comparison. The calculation uses the Woodbury identity and matches an
independent dense check. It is conditional on the selected positive-coefficient
active set and fixed instrumental calibration, and omits galaxy posterior
uncertainty. **These score differences are not calibrated significance levels
or Bayes factors.**

Positive differences mean the high-speed model predicts the withheld data worse:

| Withheld region | Raw chi-square: high minus low | Q with correlated continuum uncertainty: high minus low |
|---|---:|---:|
| Whole line window, R+C | +109.518 | **+34.378** |
| Whole line window, R | +17.632 | +27.802 |
| Whole line window, C | +91.886 | +6.577 |
| Central 15-pixel aperture, R+C | +111.692 | **+41.619** |
| Central aperture, R | +19.565 | +28.763 |
| Central aperture, C | +92.127 | +12.857 |

Four of six withheld exposures individually favor the lower solution. Both
grisms favor it when their exposures are combined. Accounting for continuum
covariance materially reduces the apparent advantage, especially in C.

## What the images and boundaries tell us

Both velocity branches can produce deceptively similar detector images. The
lower branch reproduces the main two-lobed R appearance and elongated C line;
the higher branch leaves a stronger central negative C residual. Horizontal
R and vertical C residual bands remain, consistent with uncertain continuum
subtraction. Their exact cause is not established by this test. Visual inspection
alone does not decisively distinguish these branches.

Cyan crosses mark the fitted kinematic center projected at the fitted systemic
wavelength using the exposure calibration. Open circles mark the nominal
calibrated line position. White pixels are excluded. Data/model heatmaps share
an asinh stretch; residual images show residual/ERR without continuum prediction
uncertainty in that display scale.

The lower solution's turnover radius reaches 0.02 arcsec (approximately 170 pc).
Therefore a stable velocity at 4254 pc does **not** establish the inner rotation
or mass profile. The high-speed solution reaches the existing 1000 km/s prior
ceiling; the ceiling does not make that solution a valid velocity measurement.

This experiment supports further work on the lower branch. It does not resolve
ID15665's inclination, prove that inverse rectification caused its high speeds,
validate MCMC uncertainties, or quantify improvement over a single grism.
