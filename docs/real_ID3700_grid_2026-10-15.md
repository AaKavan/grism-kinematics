# Real ID3700: velocity grid and F150W orientation constraint

This is the first application of the new native-exposure workflow to **the actual ID3700 pixels**. It is separate from the synthetic posterior experiments in [native_posterior_2026-10-15.md](native_posterior_2026-10-15.md). Both grism directions enter one joint fit throughout: nine R exposures and nine C exposures. The output root is `../../results/2026-10-15_real_ID3700/`.

## What the velocity means

The previous continuous NUTS model sampled projected speed `Vrot(0.5 arcsec) sin(i)`, turnover radius, kinematic angle, inclination, flux and a positive emission map. The proposed grid is a different inference calculation: fix a trial speed, refit nuisance parameters, and compare the resulting images and penalized scores. It complements the posterior sampler; it does not turn optimized nuisance parameters into marginalized probabilities.

For this real experiment the grid coordinate is **deprojected rotation at 0.5 arcsec**, `Vrot(0.5 arcsec)`, from 0 through 1000 km/s in 1 km/s steps. It is not the asymptotic arctangent speed. The zero-rotation control is included. A 1 km/s step is numerical sampling, not a claim of 1 km/s observational precision. A best fit at the upper limit would require extending the scan.

The field remains an arctangent rotation curve. Inclination affects both the projected amplitude and the conversion of sky positions into disk radii, so it must enter the fit; changing a mass by a post-fit `sin(i)` correction is insufficient.

## Real F150W imaging

SCI and ERR come from `4750_F150W_v05_sci.fits` and `4750_F150W_v05_err.fits` under `/home/aakavan/EDR_data/`. The 121-by-121 cutout has 0.03 arcsec pixels. Exact pixel-slice and PSF-member comparisons are recorded in [input_provenance.json](../../results/2026-10-15_real_ID3700/imaging/input_provenance.json). The PSF is the supplied empirical `SAP-EDR-PSF/4750_f150w_ePSF.fits`, sampled from 0.015 to 0.03 arcsec after measuring its core centroid. Its measured centroid is near `(51.00, 50.97)` in the 103-pixel array: there is no large centering error in the array actually used.

PySersic fits a PSF-convolved single Sérsic profile and a constant sky within 1.2 arcsec. The source is visibly clumpy, so the formal single-profile uncertainty is not a total orientation uncertainty. The comparison excludes either none of the core or the central 0.18 arcsec. Four NUTS chains retain 1000 samples each after 500 warmup iterations. Angle-boundary occupation and chain diagnostics are checked separately from model adequacy.

Three setup issues were caught before constructing the orientation constraint:

- PySersic's theta convention placed the nearly horizontal source at the cut of `[-pi/2, pi/2]`. The equivalent `[0, pi]` parameterization puts its mode in the interior. The original boundary-limited fit is preserved but excluded.
- `SourceProperties` supplies a flux-error estimate based on the square root of flux. That is inappropriate as a tight prior after normalizing an MJy/sr mosaic by its median ERR. The retained fits use an explicit broad positive flux prior and a broad sky prior. Early automatic-prior fits are preserved as diagnostics.
- The first custom prior allowed Sérsic indices below the fast `HybridRenderer` interpolation table's lower bound of 0.65. The core-masked fit preferred this region, causing nonfinite optimizer trials and 3803 divergent samples in a failed run. This was an error in this diagnostic's setup, not evidence for a physical inclination constraint. The retained fits use PySersic's direct `PixelRenderer`, `n` between 0.3 and 6, and a trust-region initializer with a stationarity check. Failed samples are excluded from the orientation distribution.

### How axis ratio informs inclination

PySersic measures an apparent axis ratio `q`, not a gas inclination. For an axisymmetric oblate disk of intrinsic thickness `q0`,

```
cos(i)^2 = (q^2 - q0^2) / (1 - q0^2).
```

An infinitely thin disk has `q0=0`. Photometric and kinematic inclinations describe the same orientation if stars and gas occupy the same plane. Clumps, bulges, dust, finite thickness and real misalignment can make the estimates differ. The imaging constraint is therefore soft; the gas inclination remains a fitted parameter.

This pilot uses an equal mixture of the full and core-masked axis-ratio samples, `q0 ~ Uniform(0.05, 0.30)`, and 10 degrees of additional Gaussian scatter in inclination, truncated to 5–85 degrees. These thickness and scatter choices are **explicit exploratory assumptions**, not measurements or a calibrated population model. The two image fits are alternative morphology descriptions of the same pixels, not independent observations. This transferred orientation distribution is not a full hierarchical imaging likelihood. A publication analysis should test the thickness, alignment, morphology and imaging-prior assumptions, including the appropriate prior/Jacobian treatment.

## Real grism likelihood and nuisance parameters

The likelihood uses original native SCI, its ERR, and fixed DQ masks, with no resampling or coaddition of observed pixels. EMLINE is not fitted with SCI errors. A quadratic continuum along each R row or C column is eliminated by weighted least squares at every trial.

The shared emission map is a positive log-brightness field on 21-by-21 controls, integrated on 61-by-61 quadrature over ±0.9 arcsec. Log brightness is interpolated rather than interpolating positive control values. Its envelope has a free width, axis ratio and angle independent of the kinematic geometry. This addresses the circular-envelope and interpolation problems identified in the synthetic work without imposing the continuum image's shape on the line emission. The field amplitude and correlation length remain specified regularization assumptions.

At each trial speed the fit adjusts turnover radius, kinematic angle, inclination, systemic velocity, intrinsic line width, kinematic center, emission-envelope parameters, map coefficients and detector-registration shifts. Each exposure has its own positive line normalization, analytically profiled, to avoid treating unverified absolute/relative sensitivity conversion as exact. These independent amplitudes preserve the shared spatial brightness shape. Registration and center shifts have explicit 0.25-pixel and 0.15-arcsec Gaussian scales, respectively; these are provisional calibration assumptions.

The spectral response uses the supplied module/grism double-Gaussian LSF calibration, with parameters evaluated as `k log10(lambda/4 microns) + b`. Across dispersion, the model uses a circularized Gaussian fit to the F444W empirical PSF core, scaled by `lambda/4.44`. No extra optical convolution is applied along the empirical spectral LSF. The 1/3-pixel-squared variance of cubic source deposition is removed from each kernel component's second moment. This preserves the intended second moments, but does not reproduce the complete empirical PSF/LSF shape. Wings, asymmetry and wavelength scaling need further validation.

The coarse search uses several velocities and four different angle starts. The integer grid is traversed in both directions, carrying and comparing nearby fits. Optimizer stationarity is recorded explicitly: a stored finite fit is not automatically a converged fit. This reduces start dependence; it cannot prove that every nuisance mode has been found.

## Interpretation and scientific limits

Visual inspection compares fixed-scale native SCI/model/residual panels for individual exposures within the **joint R+C fit**. A physical reason to reject a solution—structured residuals, an unmodeled contaminant, an incorrect response—is more informative than which picture looks preferable. Equally acceptable velocity branches remain alternatives in the inference.

The score contains both data likelihood and explicit penalties. Its differences are not automatically chi-square confidence thresholds, and exponentiating the profile is not a marginal posterior. Even a convincing preference for nonzero rotation does not by itself establish reliable dynamical-mass intervals.

For selected fits, diagnostic curves use `Mrot(r)=r Vrot(r)^2/G` with Planck18 at `z=1.1231`. This is a spherical-equivalent rotation-only mass proxy, with no pressure-support or lensing correction. Final review plots use an explicitly illustrative 0.2–0.8 arcsec radius range; this is not a measurement of observed or resolved coverage. The map-derived 10–90% flux radii are retained as diagnostics but are unreliable as an observed extent because of faint off-source/edge flux, as discussed below. Resolved radial leverage, model sensitivity and recovery coverage must be checked before reporting mass profiles as science results.

## Real-data center comparison

**Both requested center treatments prefer approximately 278 km/s at 0.5 arcsec.** Both use the same 18 real R+C exposures, F150W inclination distribution, response model and emission-map regularization. Speed and all nuisance parameters are reoptimized continuously from four saved grid solutions, at 250, 350, 500 and 1000 km/s.

The soft-center case uses a two-dimensional Gaussian, with 0.05 arcsec standard deviation per coordinate, about the mean of the full/core-masked F150W centroids. That scale allows morphology and astrometry differences; it is not the formal centroid error. The free-center case removes the Gaussian center penalty and allows each coordinate within ±0.6 arcsec of the catalog position. Thus “free” still means a finite stated search region. The soft case retains ±0.3 arcsec bounds. A separate baseline run with expanded ±0.6 arcsec bounds checks that bound choice.

The F150W centroid mean is `(east, north) = (+0.00433, +0.00233)` arcsec relative to the spectroscopic catalog origin, using the cutout WCS and the fractional source position. The two image-fit centroids differ by about 0.031 arcsec, another reason not to impose a delta-function center constraint.

Extra digits make the numerical comparison reproducible; they are **not measurement precision**. The best velocities differ by 0.21 km/s (0.075%), and the corresponding mass proxies differ by 0.15%. Their model residuals differ by only 0.00020 ERR RMS in R and 0.00022 ERR in C over the fitted pixels. The earlier concern about a ~0.3 arcsec center displacement belongs to a worse local solution, not either preferred fit. This does not independently establish perfect gas/stellar alignment: these are conditional optima under the current model.

The two low-speed starts converge to the preferred branch. The two high-speed starts retain worse solutions: approximately **431 km/s** with the soft constraint and **525 km/s** with the free center. Relative to the best fit **within the same center model**, their `2 Δ objective` values are **59.71** and **40.16**, respectively. Their north offsets are approximately −0.136 and −0.358 arcsec. These differences are not calibrated significances, and scores across different prior choices are not Bayes factors.

**Conclusion about the center:** freeing the center does not force the preferred fit to a high velocity or a large displacement. It does make a high-velocity displaced local solution easier to fit. Multiple starts and preserving competing solutions are therefore still necessary.

## What the scan revealed about starting conditions

The ascending 1 km/s grid initially preferred **497 km/s**, with its center on the −0.3 arcsec north bound. The descending scan found a better emission-map/kinematic branch at **278 km/s**. Under the same baseline target, their objective values are −6033.25 and −6056.01: the latter is better by `2 Δ objective ≈ 45.5`.

This is direct evidence for the original concern. Fixing velocity on a grid removes velocity as an optimizer starting value, but it does **not** eliminate local minima in the refitted map, center, inclination and turnover radius. Even points passing a local gradient check can belong to an inferior branch. Selecting a single optimization result or a single sweep would have produced a materially different mass proxy.

An artificial jump near 318 km/s also appeared when adjacent grid points inherited different nuisance branches. The additional outward sweep follows the newly found best branch independently across every integer speed, retaining whichever saved solution has the lowest objective at each point. Failed stationarity checks receive longer retries. A true globally optimized profile should be continuous here; an abrupt numerical jump is a search diagnostic, not evidence for a physical discontinuity. These checks improve the search without proving a global optimum.

The completed search retains **3034 fits**: 24 coarse starts, two complete 1001-point integer sweeps, 1000 additional outward continuation fits around the best point, and eight stationarity refits. All **1001 best stored integer-velocity fits pass** the explicit projected-gradient threshold of 0.1. A stricter optimizer tolerance was needed for the final 443 km/s retry; its score improved slightly, without changing the minimum. The final baseline integer-grid minimum remains **278 km/s**. The false jump near 318 km/s is removed. All frozen mathematical-code hashes remain unchanged through these search refinements.

For visual review only, grid points with `2 Δ objective ≤ 2` span 261–305 km/s and those with `≤ 10` span 245–381 km/s. These numbers describe the shape of this penalized profile; **they are not confidence or credible intervals**. In particular, the spread from the separate assumption-sensitivity tests should not be mistaken for the full velocity uncertainty.

## Remaining problem: which faint pixels belong to the disk?

The preferred flexible line map includes faint isolated blobs and edge flux beyond its bright elongated central component. About **6.8%** of its modeled flux falls in the outermost three quadrature rows/columns of the ±0.9 arcsec map. At the fitted inclination of approximately 78.5 degrees, deprojection puts the map's 90%-flux radius at about **3.1 arcsec, or 26 kpc**. That is a diagnostic of the current fitted map, **not a trustworthy observed emission radius**. The diagnostic NPZ files preserve these model-derived radii; they must not be used as science radial limits.

The photometric catalog has neighboring detections at approximately 1.53, 1.61 and 1.64 arcsec; the nearest different object in the spectroscopic catalog is about 5.02 arcsec away. These facts do not identify particular contaminating grism pixels or rule contamination out. The faint modeled emission could include real clumps, response mismatch, continuum residuals, contamination or noise being absorbed by the flexible map. Source assignment must be checked in detector space across exposures and directions before giving those blobs the same disk velocity law.

![Imaging context and fitted line emission](../../results/2026-10-15_real_ID3700/comparison/source_context.png)

The center result is therefore promising, but reliable mass constraints require more than agreement between these two centers. The next analysis should establish which emission is consistently associated with ID3700, represent contaminants separately or apply independently justified fixed masks, validate the response and residual noise, and then run the real joint posterior from the surviving kinematic branches. Radial sensitivity and injection/recovery checks should set the reported radial range. The best-fit centers can be compared now; scientifically defensible mass intervals still need those checks.

The GPU implementation profiles exposure amplitudes and continuum analytically, compiles the shared projection and gradients once, and warm-starts adjacent velocities. This makes repeated conditional fits practical. Parallel scans should use independent starts/branches rather than replace the bidirectional checks with one fast sweep.

Visual inspection can diagnose model failures; it should not erase statistically competitive alternatives just because one image is preferred.
