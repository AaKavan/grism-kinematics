# Real ID6170: first test and a line/continuum failure

**Later October 18 update:** the repeated detector pixel responsible for the extreme cancellation has now been identified and masked in six cutouts. Short masked fits of both the previous and revised models remove that cancellation. ID6170 still has a boundary-sensitive geometry and no reportable velocity or mass. See the [repair results and conclusions](shared_flux_repair_2026-10-18.md) and [new results notebook](../notebooks/archive/real_source_repaired.ipynb). The account below records the original, unmasked diagnostic and its then-proposed next steps.

ID6170 is a useful second real-source test, but **the current fit does not provide a reportable rotation velocity, dispersion, V/σ or mass profile**. It exposed a degeneracy that was not prominent in the preferred ID3700 fit: the model constructs a very bright, extended C line component and cancels it with a large negative polynomial continuum. Independent exposure normalizations allow this C component to coexist with a much fainter R prediction.

The velocity search was deliberately interrupted after preserving 77 fits. No kinematic MCMC or fixed-dispersion/response comparison was launched. The partial grid is retained as a diagnostic, not silently marked complete. The KeyboardInterrupt in its log records that deliberate stop.

Open [real_source_ID6170.ipynb](../notebooks/archive/real_source_ID6170.ipynb) in the **grism** kernel and use Run All to inspect the saved results. All fitting switches are false. It contains executed images, including separate line and continuum panels. The original ID3700 notebook and results were left in place.

## Inputs and imaging

The source has all nine R and nine C native exposures, all module B, with SCI, ERR and the existing DQ masks. No exposures were excluded. The catalog identification is RA 63.942760°, Dec −24.171430°, z=1.1147, Paα at the configured rest wavelength 1.875 μm, observed wavelength 3.9650625 μm. There are 116,309 valid native pixels in the 81×81 cutouts. The maximum WCS/STATS displacement is 0.222 pixel, within the adopted 0.25-pixel registration scale.

![All native exposures](../../results/real_sources/ID6170/inspection_2026-10-18/all_exposures.png)

F150W shows an elongated, clumpy source. Both four-chain PySersic fits passed their sampling checks: 500 warmup steps and 1,000 retained draws per chain, zero divergences and no maximum-tree-depth saturation. These are **imaging** chains; they do not constitute a kinematic MCMC fit.

| Imaging fit | Median q | Median effective radius | Maximum Rhat | Minimum bulk ESS | Residual RMS / ERR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Full fitted region | 0.3101 | 0.6254″ | 1.0022 | 2044 | 2.77 |
| Central 0.18″ excluded | 0.2668 | 0.6821″ | 1.0025 | 2176 | 1.80 |

The fits leave coherent clump residuals. Their tiny formal shape errors therefore do not describe all morphology uncertainty. The inclination prior gives equal weight to the two alternatives, uses intrinsic thickness q0 drawn uniformly from 0.05–0.30 with physically inconsistent q<q0 pairs rejected, and adds 10° of stellar/gas inclination scatter. The underlying stellar inclination mixture has 5th/50th/95th percentiles 72.5°/76.4°/84.0°. The final gas prior is broadened and truncated to 5–85°. These are provisional disk assumptions.

The nearby photometric catalog detection ID31655 is 0.67″ from the target. Catalog proximity does not establish contamination of a dispersed pixel. The C images show an adjacent bright trace, particularly in exposures 10–15; its contribution must be assessed with trace predictions and residuals, not its brightness alone.

## What the search did

The test used the existing shared log-emission-map model, 21×21 controls, 61×61 integration nodes on a ±0.9″ sky square, nominal instrument response, quadratic per-strip continuum, and the source's new imaging prior. The center prior is the same catalog-centered 0.15″ Gaussian, bounded at ±0.3″, used for the nominal ID3700 comparison.

The saved work comprises 24 coarse initializations, a full upward sweep over 0–1000 km/s at 25 km/s spacing, and 12 downward fits from 1000 to 725 km/s. Optimizer calls consumed about 524 seconds. The downward sweep, outward sweep and final retries were not completed. Five of the 41 lowest stored velocity fits pass the stationarity threshold; the lowest-score solution does not.

The lowest stored penalized objective is at **Vrot(0.5″)=900 km/s**, with σ≈28.3 km/s, inclination 85°, turnover radius 2.0″ and center (x0,y0)≈(−0.144″,+0.044″). Inclination and turnover are at their upper bounds. The projected-gradient norm is 2.94, versus the 0.1 acceptance threshold. These numbers identify the failed fit for inspection; **900 km/s and 28.3 km/s are not measurements**. No global optimum or velocity interval has been established.

## The concrete failure

![ID6170 line and continuum separately](../../results/real_sources/ID6170/decomposition_ID6170_2026-10-18/line_continuum.png)

The first C row is exposure 10; the second is exposure 16 from another detector placement. In exposure 10, a bright positive line component extends over most of the dispersion axis while a negative continuum cancels it. The R prediction does not reproduce the localized line feature well. Looking only at the combined prediction hides how the fit is assembled.

For a saved line template L, define

\[
f_{\perp}=\frac{\|P(L/\mathrm{ERR})\|^2}{\|L/\mathrm{ERR}\|^2},
\]

using valid pixels, where P projects out the allowed polynomial continuum. A small value means the predicted line closely resembles a continuum component. **This is a noise-weighted squared-norm fraction, not a fraction of emission flux.**

| Saved fit diagnostic | ID6170, 900 km/s failed solution | ID3700, preferred 278 km/s grid solution |
|---|---:|---:|
| Median C amplitude / median R amplitude | 324.33 | 1.077 |
| R fitted amplitude range | 0.76–2.01 | 2.82–3.21 |
| C fitted amplitude range | 24.81–458.14 | 2.91–3.51 |
| R template power remaining after continuum projection | 63.9–64.6% | 63.3–64.4% |
| C template power remaining after continuum projection | 0.190–0.300% | 77.6–78.7% |

The current projector uses unit exposure response and profiles a separate nonnegative amplitude in each exposure. These numerical amplitudes have not been corrected for relative throughput. Their inconsistency, combined with the explicit negative continuum compensation, establishes a model-decomposition problem. It is not evidence that C observes a physically brighter galaxy or a different dispersion.

The available F444W module-B sensitivity files give C/R **point spectral sensitivity** ratios of about 0.996 at ID6170's wavelength and 1.003 at ID3700's wavelength. They are in DN/s/Jy. An integrated-line conversion still needs pixel wavelength intervals and exposure/spatial calibration; those point ratios have not been applied to these fits. They provide no indication of a throughput difference remotely comparable to a factor of 324. File hashes and interpolation values are saved in [sensitivity_available.json](../../results/real_sources/ID6170/inspection_2026-10-18/sensitivity_available.json).

The new [decomposition viewer](../dingo/real_decomposition.py) reads cached fits without refitting. Its per-exposure residual sums reproduce the grid's stored χ² and the continuum-projected likelihood independently. For ID6170, χ² contributions are 61,436.69 from R and 103,290.04 from C. These are components of one joint fit, not separate R-only/C-only fits or calibrated significance statistics.

## What the continuum check does and does not establish

A separate check fitted continuum only outside ±20 dispersion pixels and predicted a held-out quarter of those sideband pixels. The prediction variance was included with ERR. For quadratic continuum:

| Source / grism | Held-out robust scatter | Held-out RMS | Fraction beyond ±5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| ID6170 R | 1.001 | 1.023 | 0 |
| ID6170 C | 1.009 | 1.237 | 0.00227 |
| ID3700 R | 1.013 | 1.032 | 0.00025 |
| ID3700 C | 1.122 | 1.155 | 0.00038 |

Most ID6170 sideband pixels have a reasonable pointwise noise scale; C has larger outliers. The adjacent bright trace is largely represented by the continuum in this check. This does **not** validate extrapolation under the target line or rule out contamination. Shared continuum prediction errors also correlate held-out pixels. Degrees 1 and 3 were checked as well; simply raising polynomial order does not remove the physical line/continuum ambiguity.

## Next changes, before long chains

1. **Calibrate and couple the line normalizations.** Use one source line flux multiplied by the known exposure response and a justified allowance for relative calibration differences. The needed R/C sensitivity tables are already in the copied calibration directory. Check pixel wavelength widths, units, spatial dependence and the calibration versions first. Allowing 18 unrelated amplitudes weakens the physical link between the two grism directions.
2. **Constrain the continuum physically.** Model the background uncertainty and continuum so a huge negative component cannot cancel an arbitrarily bright line. A nonnegative continuum representation with a separately justified background offset, or a proper continuum prior, is worth testing. Do not blindly require every background-subtracted SCI pixel to be positive. If sidebands inform a prior, avoid counting those same pixels again as independent evidence.
3. **Audit the discrepant C exposure groups and source assignment.** Exposures 10–15 and 16–18 sample different detector placements and favor very different line amplitudes in the failed solution. Compare calibrated target fluxes and traced neighboring sources. Mask or model confirmed contamination using independent evidence, rather than deleting pixels because they prefer an inconvenient velocity.
4. **Repeat ID6170 and ID3700 with the repaired model.** Compare observed/predicted/residual images, separate line/continuum components, exposure normalizations and multiple starting branches. Then run the fixed-dispersion response checks and fresh four-chain MCMC. Additional parameter bounds alone would merely hide this failure.

The present experiment cannot tell us whether ID6170 has a different intrinsic dispersion from ID3700. It also does not show that joint R+C kinematics cannot work. It shows that the current flexible map, unconstrained continuum and independent flux amplitudes permit a physically unsuitable solution. The same model components exist in ID3700, so its conditional results should also be rechecked after repair, even though its preferred fit does not exhibit this particular cancellation.
